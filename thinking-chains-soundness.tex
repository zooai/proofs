\begin{abstract}
We record an adversarial review of the \emph{Thinking Chains} (Proof of AI)
stack: the on-chain Tier-0 governance contracts (\texttt{ThinkingGovernor},
\texttt{ProofOfThoughtRegistry}, \texttt{ThinkingChainObservatory},
\texttt{GovernancePoTBridge}, \texttt{ThinkingReputation}, \texttt{L3Registry},
\texttt{AICoin}), the off-chain operator (Go) that signs verdicts in parity with
the Solidity contracts, the DAO visibility dashboard, the local two-chain proof
harness, and the formal/economic claims of the two companion papers. Five
independent adversarial reviewers attacked five disjoint surfaces. No theft,
consensus-compromise, or cap-violation was found; the conceptual core
(deterministic replay over authenticated artifacts; the $10^9$ capped, halving,
provably-bounded issuance) held under analysis and fuzzing. The review did find,
and we fixed, real integrity bugs on the \emph{visibility} layer (an
unauthenticated receipt ledger, a deterministic-id front-run, a dashboard
arithmetic DoS, a stored XSS, a proof harness that displayed rather than
asserted), a cross-instance/cross-chain signature-replay gap (missing EIP-712
domain separation), and several \emph{overstated economic-security claims} in the
papers (a Sybil-inviting routing exponent, a quorum bound valid only for
equal-vote committees, a reputation rule mis-described as a proper scoring rule,
and a subsidy that did not require provable work). All contract fixes are covered
by tests (135/135) and a live end-to-end proof (9/9 on-chain assertions); all
paper claims are tightened or downgraded to defensible statements. This document
is the durable record of what was attacked, what held, what changed, and the
residual trust assumptions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and method}

\paragraph{System under review.} A Thinking Chain settles cognition and mints a
coin for it. Two layers are in scope here. \textbf{Tier-0} is a set of
EVM-agnostic Solidity contracts (the DAO governance: operator-LLM quorum decides
on-chain knobs; a deterministic receipt ledger; a reputation EMA; the native
coin's issuance) deployed to any EVM. \textbf{Tier-1} is the in-protocol AI
precompile (a deterministic int8 inference path) implemented in the Rust
revm/reth execution client; the precompile's inference correctness is out of
scope for this review, but its determinism is the premise that makes
``deterministic recomputation'' a valid evidence type (Section~\ref{sec:econ}).
The off-chain operator that reads the chain and signs verdicts is Go (the Zoo-L3
node path), and is held in byte-parity with the Solidity digest by golden
vectors.

\paragraph{Threat model.} Adversaries may: open governance thoughts with
arbitrary parameters; bond and submit signed verdicts; call any public method
from any address; deploy duplicate or malicious contracts; front-run; and supply
malicious on-chain strings consumed by off-chain readers. They may not break
secp256k1, keccak256, or the BFT consensus the chain runs under. The
inference precompile and the engine are trusted to be deterministic where claimed.

\paragraph{Method.} Five independent reviewers attacked disjoint surfaces:
(R1) \texttt{AICoin} issuance; (R2) \texttt{ThinkingGovernor}; (R3) the
observatory and auxiliary contracts; (R4) the papers' theorems and economic
mechanism; (R5) the proof harness, deploy scripts, and dashboard. Each was
required to produce concrete proof-of-concept (a failing test or explicit call
sequence), to default to skepticism, and to state plainly what it could
\emph{not} break. Findings below are de-duplicated and dispositioned.

\section{On-chain invariants (held; now test-pinned)}

The following are the load-bearing safety properties. Each was attacked and held;
each is now pinned by a named test in the \texttt{luxdao/contracts} suite
(135 tests green) and, where end-to-end, by the local two-chain proof
(\texttt{beluga-l3-live.sh}, 9/9 assertions).

\begin{proposition}[Issuance cap]
For all timestamps and all sequences of \texttt{mintSubsidy} calls, cumulative
minted subsidy never exceeds $S_{\max}=10^{9}\,\textsc{ai}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Argument]
Minting is bounded by \texttt{emissionAllowance()} $=$
\texttt{cumulativeAllowance()} $-$ \texttt{mintedSubsidy}. The schedule
$\mathrm{allowed}(t)=S_{\max}\!\left(1-2^{-k}\right)+f\,S_{\max}2^{-(k+1)}$,
$k=\lfloor t/T_h\rfloor$, $f\in[0,1)$, is continuous, strictly increasing, and
bounded above by $S_{\max}$ (the per-epoch emissions $S_{\max}2^{-(k+1)}$ sum to
$S_{\max}$); the floor rounding of the intra-epoch term is conservative
(undershoots). Hence $\texttt{mintedSubsidy}\le\mathrm{allowed}(t)\le S_{\max}$.
Verified by exact-integer modelling over $k\in[0,128]$ at one-second resolution
and by a 2000-run sequential fuzz (\texttt{testFuzz\_SequentialCapWalk});
over-minting by one wei reverts at every sampled timestamp.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Supply $\le$ minted; dashboard cannot underflow]
\texttt{totalSupply} $\le$ \texttt{mintedSubsidy} always, so the observatory's
$\texttt{burned}=\texttt{minted}-\texttt{circulating}$ is non-negative.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Argument]
\texttt{mintedSubsidy} increases only inside \texttt{mintSubsidy}, by the same
amount passed to \texttt{\_mint}; the only supply-reducer is \texttt{burn}; there
is no other mint path (OpenZeppelin \texttt{\_mint} is internal and not
otherwise exposed). As defence in depth against a \emph{non-canonical}
\texttt{IAICoin} wired into the observatory, \texttt{economics()} now clamps
$\texttt{burned}=\max(0,\texttt{minted}-\texttt{circulating})$ rather than risk an
underflow revert that would DoS the entire dashboard.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Receipt authenticity]
Only authorized recorders may write a Proof-of-Thought receipt; a receipt cannot
be forged by an arbitrary caller, nor can its deterministic id be front-run to
block or poison a legitimate governance receipt.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Argument]
\texttt{register} is gated by an \texttt{isRecorder} allow-list under a DAO
\texttt{admin}; the \texttt{GovernancePoTBridge} is the authorized recorder for
governance receipts. Before this fix \texttt{register} was permissionless, which
allowed (i) receipts attributing paid cognition to arbitrary payer/operator and
inflating the dashboard's volume metric, and (ii) a front-run of the
deterministic \texttt{receiptId} (all inputs public) that permanently blocked the
bridge's write and injected a forged \texttt{quorumProof}. Gating writers closes
both. Pinned by \texttt{test\_Register\_OnlyRecorder}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Verdict signature domain separation]
A verdict signature is valid only for the governor instance and chain it was
produced for.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Argument]
The signed digest now binds \texttt{block.chainid} and \texttt{address(this)} in
addition to the purpose domain, task, model spec, vote, bucket, evidence, and
operator. Without this, an operator's own re-submission of a byte-identical
signature could manufacture a quorum on a fork, an L2, or a same-address twin
(the prior \texttt{signer==msg.sender} check blocked third-party relay but not
self-replay across instances). Go operator mirror and the cross-language golden
vector were updated in lockstep (chainId $808080$, verifying contract
\texttt{0x..beEF}); the contract layout is pinned by
\texttt{test\_GoldenVerdictDigestParity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Settlement robustness]
The knob mirror to the DAO key/value singleton is now gas-capped
(\texttt{\{gas:200{,}000\}}) so a gas-\emph{burning} (vs reverting) singleton
cannot brick or tax \texttt{settle} via the 63/64 rule. The \texttt{L3Registry}
gained a DAO-gated \texttt{reassign} to recover a squatted chainId and a
zero-address guard on the authority transfer.
\end{remark}

\section{Economic-security caveats (now corrected in the papers)}
\label{sec:econ}

The review's sharpest contribution was on the \emph{economic} claims, which were
stated too strongly. The corrected, defensible positions:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Sybil-resistance is a cost, not an exponent.} A concave bond weight
$B^\alpha$ ($\alpha<1$) is subadditive, so splitting one bond across $m$
identities yields $m^{1-\alpha}B^\alpha>B^\alpha$: concavity \emph{rewards}
fragmentation. Resistance must come from a per-identity registration cost and
stake floor (splitting net-negative) and a per-identity committee-weight cap,
specified in the Zoo DAO paper---not from the exponent.

\item \textbf{Quorum capture is exponentially rare only for equal-vote
committees.} The bound $\exp(-2n(\theta-p)^2)$ holds when each seat is one
equal vote sampled independently of weight. Under weight-proportional sampling
and weighted certification, a sub-threshold heavy node can capture with
probability near $1$. Safety-critical quorums therefore certify by equal seats;
weighted committees require a per-seat weight cap and yield a strictly weaker,
cap-dependent guarantee.

\item \textbf{Subsidy requires provable work.} A signed finite judgment is free
to produce, so minting cannot be gated on a bare quorum signature without
inviting work-free coinage. Subsidy minting is gated on provable-work evidence
(deterministic recomputation of the int8 model, attestation, or zkML); quorum-only
judgments earn demand fees but do not mint. This restores the Bitcoin-style
cost-asymmetry (``no coin without a thought'') for cognition.

\item \textbf{Reputation is coordination, not truth.} The EMA rewards agreement
with the canonical (quorum) outcome, which is not ground truth; for subjective
tasks it is not by itself incentive-compatible for honest minority reports. The
``slash liars, not dissenters'' principle is sound as a \emph{confiscation}
policy (dissent is never slashed); truth-elicitation for subjective,
money-moving decisions uses a proper scoring rule against later-revealed outcomes
or a bonded dispute game.

\item \textbf{Issuance cap is a supremum.} $M(t)\uparrow S_{\max}$ strictly and
never reaches $10^9$ over any finite horizon; in integer base units the schedule
floors to zero in finite time leaving a negligible dust remainder below the cap.
\end{itemize}

What \emph{held} on the conceptual side: the determinism result
(deterministic replay over authenticated artifacts) is sound and resolves the
``LLMs are nondeterministic, so you cannot replay'' objection---consensus
consumes committed artifact bytes via deterministic predicates, never a live
generation; the recursive-upgrade-termination and training-provenance-audit
results are correctly proven; and the single-evidence receipt-soundness bound is
a valid restatement of the verifier's trust assumption.

\section{Permissionless-network readiness (second red round)}
\label{sec:permissionless}

A second adversarial round asked the deployment question directly: is this safe on a
\emph{public permissionless} network where anyone may register (pay \texttt{minBond}),
open rounds, submit, and settle, and where an attacker can cheaply spin up many
bonded identities and front-run? Three independent reviewers converged, with
proof-of-concept, on a single fatal blocker.

\begin{theorem}[Permissionless committee-capture blocker, pre-fix]
With \emph{recoverable} bonds and \emph{first-come} committees (no sampling), an
adversary captures any committee---and thus any governed knob or median---for a sunk
cost of at most one open fee, independent of committee size: register $n$ Sybil
identities (cost $n\cdot\texttt{minBond}$, fully refundable), fill the committee, sign
the desired outcome, settle, then reclaim every bond and (being the agreeing group)
the reward escrow.
\end{theorem}

The PoCs demonstrated arbitrary knob capture on \texttt{ThinkingGovernor} (net sunk
cost $=$ open fee, bonds + reward recovered) and \emph{free} median capture on
\texttt{ThinkingParameters} (no fee, no cap, no sampling). Root cause: the median's
``robust to any minority $<50\%$'' guarantee assumes a fixed honest-majority
committee, which permissionless entry voids; a recoverable bond is a deposit, not a
sunk Sybil cost.

\paragraph{Fix (no-slashing-of-dissent honored).} Two changes, structural and
economic, neither of which slashes a dissenter:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Cryptographic sortition} (\texttt{Sortition.sol}): each round's
committee is sampled by $H(\mathrm{seed}\,\|\,i)<2^{256}n/N$ with a seed fixed
\emph{after} registration and round-open (a future block hash), so committee share
tracks population share and capturing a committee needs a \emph{population majority}
(core paper, Thm.~``Sortition capture requires a population majority''; verified in
\texttt{Sortition.t.sol}: a 40\% adversary stays a committee minority, 70\% gains it).
\item \textbf{A non-refundable registration / participation fee} (a \emph{sunk}
per-identity cost---never a slash): acquiring a population majority then costs
$\Omega(N)$ in sunk capital.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Status by contract.} \texttt{ThinkingParameters} (the most exposed
surface---free median capture) is \emph{fixed and proven}: \texttt{submitProposal}
now requires the proposer be registered before the round opened \emph{and}
sortition-sampled; the committee is bounded to $n$; a non-refundable
\texttt{proposalFee}/\texttt{openFee} (sunk to treasury) prices each seat and deters
spam; a minimum window blocks flash open-and-settle lockout. \texttt{ThinkingGovernor}
received the additive sortition \emph{foundations} (\texttt{operatorCount},
\texttt{operatorSince}, registration block) with zero behavior change (84 tests stay
green); its vote-committee receives the identical sortition + non-refundable
registration fee as the immediate next step (the pattern is validated on
\texttt{ThinkingParameters}). Suite: 156 tests green; the value-decision path is
live-proven on the two-chain stack with the sortition flow.

\paragraph{Honest readiness statement.} The value-deciding path is permissionless-safe.
The vote-deciding governor is safe under sortition once its (already-scaffolded)
committee sampling + registration fee land; until then it is safe behind an
allowlisted operator set. No theft, consensus break, or cap violation exists in
either; the gap was committee \emph{capture}, now closed structurally for values and
scaffolded for votes.

\section{Residual trust assumptions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Minter custody.} \texttt{AICoin} enforces the issuance envelope
(cap + halving) on-chain; \emph{fairness} of distribution is delegated to the
settlement contract authorized as minter and to the \texttt{admin} key. The cap
bounds the blast radius of a compromised minter to no more than the honest
schedule would have emitted.
\item \textbf{Deterministic precompile.} ``Deterministic recomputation''
evidence ($\eta_e=0$) is valid only for the fixed-point/int8 inference path with
a canonical execution spec; floating-point inference on heterogeneous hardware
must use signed-judgment or attestation evidence.
\item \textbf{Composition.} \texttt{ThinkingChainObservatory},
\texttt{ThinkingGate}, and \texttt{ThinkingReputation} accept arbitrary
governor/coin implementations; their guarantees are stated against the canonical
contracts. L3s wiring their own implementations inherit the obligation to meet
the same invariants.
\item \textbf{BFT and cryptography.} secp256k1, keccak256, and the underlying
post-quantum BFT consensus are assumed unbroken.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

The Thinking Chains spine is sound: issuance cannot exceed its cap, the receipt
ledger is now authenticated, verdict signatures are domain-separated, and the
deterministic-replay guarantee holds. The defects the review surfaced were
concentrated where new surface was added---the visibility layer and the economic
claims---and have been fixed (contracts, with tests) or tightened (papers, to
defensible statements). The honest headline: the cryptographic and
state-machine core is publishable as-is; the economic-security mechanism is sound
only with the Sybil-cost, equal-vote-quorum, provable-work, and dispute-game
provisions now made explicit. The bound that matters most---a coin is minted only
by verifiably-performed, capped, halving cognition---survives.
